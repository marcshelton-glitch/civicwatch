01 — Brand Overview

Who we are.

CivicWatch exists to make congressional financial activity radically transparent. Our brand reflects that mission: direct, urgent, trustworthy — never sensational.

Brand personality
Urgent
We speak plainly. The facts demand attention — no editorializing needed.
Factual
Every claim links to a source. We never speculate or imply what the data doesn't say.
Empowering
We hand power to citizens. The tool does the work; the user decides what to do with it.
Trustworthy
We hold ourselves to the same standard we hold Congress. Honest. Consistent. Verified.
Core mission
"Your representatives work for you. Start holding them to it."
This is our tagline, our north star, and the lens for every content decision.
What we are not
Not partisan
We cover Republicans and Democrats equally. Outrage is not the product — information is.
Not sensational
Facts speak loudly on their own. We don't editorialize, accuse, or assume guilt from data.
Not entertainment
We are a civic tool, not a news feed. Engagement comes from usefulness, not outrage.
Not inaccessible
Anyone should be able to look up their rep in 10 seconds. No account. No jargon.

06 — Motion Principles

How things move.

Motion reinforces meaning. A BUY stamp should feel impactful. Data should emerge deliberately. Animations guide attention — they never decorate for decoration's sake.

Trade amounts count up from $0 over 800ms. Makes the scale of money feel earned.

Trend lines draw left to right via stroke-dashoffset. 1 second, ease timing.
stroke-dashoffset: 300 → 0
ease · 1000ms · CSS animation
Motion rules
Duration 300–500ms for micro-interactions. 800–1200ms for data reveals. Never over 2s for a single element.
Spring easing (cubic-bezier 0.34,1.56,0.64,1) for stamps and pops. Ease for fades and slides. Linear for data counts.
Stagger sequences by 200–250ms between elements. Never animate everything simultaneously.
Loops must include a hold period (3–5s of static) so viewers can read the data before it resets.
07 — Voice & Tone

How we write.

Our voice is the same in every context — clear, direct, civic. Our tone adjusts slightly: more urgent in social content, more measured in data labels, warmer in onboarding.

Tone by context
Social Content
Urgent · Direct · Factual
Lead with the most surprising true fact. Short sentences. No hedging. The data is the punch — don't soften it.
Data Labels & UI
Precise · Neutral · Sourced
Labels describe exactly what they are. Include the source. Avoid adjectives. "Reported Net Worth" not "Suspicious Net Worth."
Onboarding & Help
Warm · Simple · Empowering
The user is new to this. Meet them where they are. Plain language. Action-first. "Enter your zip code" not "Locate your congressional district."
Writing rules
1
Lead with the fact, not the framing. "Rep. Smith bought $50,000 in NVDA. He voted against chip oversight legislation 3 days later." Not "In a questionable move, Rep. Smith may have profited from his vote."
2
Always cite the source. Every data point traces to House Ethics Office, Senate STOCK Act filings, or congressional voting records. Name the source — it's the whole point.
3
Never editorialize trade correlation as guilt. Show the data. Highlight the timing. Let the reader decide. We don't write "corrupt" or "illegal" — we show disclosures and vote records.
4
Short. Then shorter. Our social cards fit 60 words max. If a headline can lose a word, it should. "Net worth grew 340%" beats "Reported net worth may have increased by as much as 340%."
5
Equal scrutiny, no political bias. When building content, we cover Republicans and Democrats in equal measure. The design system is colorblind to party affiliation — only the data matters.
08 — Do & Don't

Examples in practice.

Real examples from cards, captions, and UI labels — showing the difference between on-brand and off-brand execution.

✓ Do — Headline
"How did net worth grow 340% on a $174,000 salary?"
The question implies significance without asserting guilt. The data speaks. Reader infers.
✗ Don't — Headline
"Corrupt Senator Enriched by Insider Knowledge"
Asserts legal conclusion without trial. Partisan in tone. Kills credibility with centrist readers.
✓ Do — Data label
"VOTED AGAINST · H.R.7842 — Tech Accountability Act · March 3, 2026"
Factual. Sourced. Uses the bill's official name. Timestamped. Reader can verify independently.
✗ Don't — Data label
"BLOCKED important tech safety bill to protect big donations"
Adds motivation the data doesn't show. We have the vote and the trade — not the reason.
✓ Do — CTA copy
"Find your rep's trades in 10 seconds."
Action-first. Specific time promise. No jargon. Empowers the user immediately.
✗ Don't — CTA copy
"Explore congressional financial transparency tools and disclosures"
Bureaucratic. No verb. No urgency. Sounds like a government website, not a civic startup.
✓ Do — Visual design
Dark background · Green accent for BUY · Red accent for opposition votes · System font
Consistent, readable, fast-loading. Works at every size from 1080×1080 social card to mobile screen.
✗ Don't — Visual design
Light backgrounds · Multiple competing accent colors · Custom external fonts · Drop shadows on text
Breaks dark-first system. Multiple colors dilute the signal value of green and red. External fonts add load time.
✓ Do — Social caption
"Rep. Rivera voted NO on the Tech Accountability Act. The next day, he bought $250K in Google stock. Both are public record. Source: House Ethics. civicwatch.app"
Sequential, factual, sourced. Reader can check every claim. No opinion added.
✗ Don't — Social caption
"🚨 CAUGHT: Rep. Rivera used insider knowledge to enrich himself while selling out his constituents. RT if disgusted. 🤮"
Asserts legal conclusion. Emotional manipulation. No source. We'd be a misinformation vector, not a transparency tool.
"When in doubt, ask: does this add information, or does it add opinion? We add information."
CivicWatch editorial standard
